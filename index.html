HIS STORY

From the academy to the village wall

Jamini Roy was born on 11 April 1887 in Beliatore village, Bankura district, in what is now West Bengal. He grew up in a family with strong artistic leanings, which shaped his interests from early childhood.

He studied formally at the Government College of Art and Craft in Kolkata, where the curriculum centred on Western realism and portraiture — the dominant "correct" style of the colonial art world at the time.

Roy grew convinced that an art true to India had to grow from its own ground, not an imported one. He turned to Kalighat pat paintings and the rural craft traditions of Bengal, gradually replacing realism with bold outline and flat colour.

His mature work settled into bold lines, bright flat colour and simplified forms — drawing on the Ramayana and Mahabharata, mother-and-child scenes, village life, and the Santhal community, among other subjects.

He deliberately avoided costly materials, working instead with natural pigments and simple, indigenous techniques — keeping his paintings within reach of ordinary people rather than only collectors.

The Government of India honoured him with the Padma Bhushan for his contribution to Indian art — recognition that arrived alongside growing international appreciation for his originality.

Jamini Roy died on 24 April 1972. He is remembered today as the artist who brought Bengal's folk traditions into the mainstream of modern Indian art, opening a path that later generations of artists continued to follow.
